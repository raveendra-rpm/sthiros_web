My documentation isn't strong enough to defend my position
When disputes happen, my daily records, site diaries, and correspondence aren't organised well enough to win the argument.

Building the paper trail that wins the argument

When a dispute happens, the subcontractor who wins is usually the one with the better documentation, not the one who was actually right, and most subcontractors don't keep daily site diaries, photographic records and correspondence consistently enough under execution pressure to matter. We design the documentation system and, more usefully, the discipline to keep it running when the job is moving too fast to feel worth stopping for.

I can't see my real margin until the project is finished
By the time I understand what a project actually made, I've already committed to the next one at a similar price.

Knowing the margin while the job is still running

Most subcontractors find out what a project actually made at the final account, months after it finished, by which point the next job is already committed at a similar price. It's a structural problem — cost tracking that lags, variation revenue that isn't recognised until approved, overhead allocated by guesswork. We rebuild the project accounting model so real margin is visible while the job is still running, not after.
